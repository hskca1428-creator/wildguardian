# Snake bite: call 000 and follow official first-aid guidance

> **Emergency: call Triple Zero (000) now and ask for an ambulance for any suspected snake bite.** Treat it as potentially life-threatening, even when the person feels well or the bite mark is hard to see. Follow the 000 operator’s instructions. [1] [2]

A snake may bite without leaving obvious fang marks, and a painless or minor-looking wound does not tell you whether venom has entered the body. Do not wait to identify the snake or look for symptoms before calling 000. [1] [3]

## Why WildGuardian does not write the steps

First aid for snake bite is a physical technique, and the official guidance is detailed and can be updated. A website cannot watch what you are doing or answer questions as you go. The 000 operator can, and Australian first-aid training teaches the technique hands-on. So this page gets you to the right help rather than giving instructions of our own.

## What to do

1. **Call 000 and ask for an ambulance.** Say it is a suspected snake bite and give your location. [1] [3]
2. **Follow the operator’s instructions.** They can talk you through first aid while the ambulance is on its way. [2]
3. **Stay with the person.** If you are on your own, call 000 first. [1]
4. **Do not try to catch or kill the snake.** Identification must never delay the call, and trying to catch a snake risks another bite. [1] [2]

## Where the official first-aid guidance is

- **ANZCOR Guideline 9.4.1** covers first-aid management of Australian snake bite. [2]
- **ANZCOR Guideline 9.4.8** covers the pressure immobilisation technique used for some Australian bites and stings. [4]
- **Healthdirect** explains snake bites, symptoms and when to seek help. [1]
- **NSW Agency for Clinical Innovation** publishes clinical information on snake and spider bite. [3]
- **Ambulance Victoria** publishes snake-awareness advice. [5]

These are written by the organisations responsible for Australian first-aid and health guidance. WildGuardian is not affiliated with any of them.

## Get trained

Techniques like pressure immobilisation are easier to apply correctly when you have practised them. A current first-aid course from an accredited provider is the best preparation if you spend time in the bush, on farms or on the water.

## Practical questions

### What if I am not sure it was a snake bite?

Treat a possible snake bite as an emergency and call 000. Bite marks may be faint, absent or mistaken for scratches, and symptoms may not be obvious at first. [1] [2]

### Does it matter what kind of snake it was?

Not for the call. You do not need to identify the snake before calling 000. Hospital clinicians work out whether venom was involved. [1]

### Does the same advice apply to spiders, jellyfish or other animals?

No. Different animals have different official guidance. See the animal’s page on WildGuardian for a link to the right official source, and call 000 if you think the situation is serious. [4]

### What if the person is alone or help cannot reach them?

Call 000 first if you can and follow the operator’s instructions. The official ANZCOR guideline covers what a person alone should do. [2]
