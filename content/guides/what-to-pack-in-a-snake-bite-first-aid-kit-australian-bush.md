# Snake-bite emergency preparation for the Australian bush

> **Emergency: a possible snake bite is a medical emergency. Call Triple Zero (000) immediately and ask for an ambulance.** Follow the operator’s instructions. Do not wait for symptoms or a snake identification. [1] [2]

In the bush, the hardest part of a snake-bite emergency is often reaching help quickly: getting a phone signal, describing where you are, and being found. This page covers that preparation. It does not give first-aid instructions. For what to do for the bite itself, use the official guidance linked below, and get hands-on training. [1] [3]

## First-aid equipment and training

Which first-aid items to carry, and how to use them, is set by the official guidance and by accredited first-aid training. WildGuardian does not write that list. Start with:

- **ANZCOR Guideline 9.4.1**, first-aid management of Australian snake bite. [1]
- **ANZCOR Guideline 9.4.8**, the pressure immobilisation technique. [3]
- **Healthdirect**, snake bites. [2]

Correct technique is difficult to get right under stress, and training improves performance. [1] [3] A current first-aid course from an accredited provider is the best preparation, and a better use of money than extra gadgets.

## Communication and trip-planning items

These items are not treatments, but they support a fast call and an accurate location.

- **A charged phone with location capability and backup power.** Call 000; if 000 does not work from a mobile, try 112. Emergency+ uses built-in smartphone GPS to help callers provide location details. [2] [4]
- **A registered GPS personal locator beacon (PLB) for remote travel, carried on the body.** Activate it only in a life-threatening situation where two-way communication is unavailable; keep its registration, contacts and battery current. [5]
- **Paper map, navigation backup and route details.** AMSA recommends paper maps as a backup to electronic navigation. Note the route, access point and a practical meeting place for responders. [5]
- **A trip plan left with a responsible contact.** Include group, route, dates and times, camps or deviations, vehicles and PLB identifier. The contact can notify police if the party is overdue and uncontactable, but the form does not guarantee an automatic search. [6]
- **A pre-trip check of weather, conditions and closures.** [5] [4]

A PLB is not an alternative to calling 000 when a call can be made. [5]

## If a possible bite happens

Call 000 and ask for an ambulance. Tell them it is a suspected snake bite and give the best location you can. Follow the operator’s instructions. [1] [2] Do not try to catch or kill the snake. [1]

## Before each trip

Check that your phone, maps, trip plan and any beacon are ready. AMSA advises reviewing safety equipment each trip and keeping beacon details current. [5] Make sure companions know who will call 000 and how the route can be described.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a personal locator beacon a replacement for a phone?

No. A PLB is a backup for a life-threatening situation when two-way communication is unavailable. If you can call, call 000; keep the beacon registered and current. [5]

### Does WildGuardian recommend specific first-aid items?

No. Use the official ANZCOR and Healthdirect guidance, and ask an accredited first-aid training provider what to carry for your trip. [1] [2]
